Load Ratings

Understanding Load Classes

We follow the EN 124 load classification system so you can match the right cover to the right traffic.

A15

Pedestrian areas and gardens only — footpaths, lawns, and pedestrianised courtyards.

B125

Footpaths and residential driveways with occasional light vehicle traffic.

C250

Kerbside zones and parking areas subject to regular car and light-vehicle traffic.

D400

Carriageways and roads carrying regular heavy-vehicle and truck traffic.

Getting It Right

Installation Guidelines

  • Bed the frame evenly on a concrete haunch — an uneven seat is the most common cause of a "rocking" cover.
  • Match the load class to expected traffic before installation — don't use an A15 cover anywhere a vehicle might drive over it.
  • Apply a thin grease film on hinge pins for hinged variants to keep them opening smoothly.
  • Inspect the rubber seal annually in odour-sensitive sewage applications and replace if compressed or cracked.
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the load class actually mean?

It's the EN 124 rating for how much weight a cover can safely bear. A15 suits pedestrian areas only, while D400 is rated for full carriageway and heavy-vehicle traffic. Using an under-rated cover in a driveway or road is a safety risk.

Hinged or non-hinged — which should I choose?

Hinged covers are easier and safer to open for frequent access chambers (utility, telecom), since the cover can't be fully removed and dropped. Non-hinged covers are simpler and cheaper where access is infrequent.

What coating options are available?

Standard covers ship with a bitumen-based protective coating. Galvanised and epoxy-coated finishes are available on request for coastal or high-corrosion environments.

What's the lead time for a municipal bulk order?
